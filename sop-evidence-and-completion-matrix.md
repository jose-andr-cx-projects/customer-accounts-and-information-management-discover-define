# SOP Evidence and Completion Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix controls the completion of the current-state SOP discovery for:

1. Customer Data and Systems Support
2. Customer Focus Information Support

It identifies what remains uncertain, the most useful evidence sources, and the smallest set of discovery activities needed to complete a credible current-state baseline.

The matrix does not confirm that every current SOP candidate exists as a separate process. Discovery may show that candidates should be combined, reframed, retained as cross-cutting controls or removed from the current-state inventory.

## Current position

All 16 SOP candidates are structured discovery drafts.

They contain plausible process hypotheses and useful validation questions, but ownership, triggers, decision rules, systems, handoffs, controls, exceptions and frequency remain largely unconfirmed.

Until supported by current-state evidence, each SOP remains `Draft current-state` and must not be represented as validated operational practice.

## Evidence principles

- Map work that happens today, including informal and inconsistent practice.
- Start from a real recent example wherever possible.
- Distinguish observed practice, reported practice and documented procedure.
- Treat position descriptions as role intent, not proof of current practice.
- Record disagreement or variation instead of forcing a single clean pathway.
- Do not reproduce customer information, personal information or restricted operational content.
- Use anonymised descriptions of evidence in the repository.
- Preserve unresolved uncertainty using `To validate`, `Unknown` or `Not confirmed`.

## Evidence types

| Code | Evidence type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| DOC | Existing documentation | Work instructions, templates, guidance, role documents, system notes or approved process material. |
| ART | Work artefact | Anonymised request, tracker structure, queue, template, communication or completed output showing the work in practice. |
| OBS | Observation | Direct observation of the officer completing or reconstructing a real task. |
| OFF | Officer account | Informal conversation or walkthrough with the officer performing the work. |
| INT | Internal-customer account | Input from a person who requests, receives, relies on or is affected by the work. |
| SYS | System evidence | Screen, field, queue, report, permission or workflow behaviour demonstrated without retaining sensitive data. |

## Confidence guide

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low | Based mainly on role documentation, assumptions or an untested process hypothesis. |
| Medium | Supported by a real example and officer account, but important decisions, variations or dependencies remain untested. |
| High | Triangulated across practice evidence, officer confirmation and a relevant document, system demonstration or internal-customer account. Important uncertainty is visible. |

High confidence does not mean the process is well designed or consistently followed. It means the current-state description is well supported.

## Minimum completion standard

An SOP can move to `In review` when the following are captured:

- confirmation that the activity occurs and is operationally meaningful;
- at least one real, recent and anonymised work example;
- normal trigger, inputs, steps and completion point;
- actual systems and tools used;
- important decisions and the rules or judgement used;
- known exceptions and workarounds;
- handoffs, approvals and escalation pathways;
- current controls and evidence retained;
- frequency or demand pattern, described qualitatively if no reliable count exists;
- current owner, maintainer and reviewer, or an explicit governance gap;
- internal-customer input where the workflow crosses team boundaries; and
- remaining uncertainty recorded in the SOP.

An SOP can move to `Validated current state` only after the officer who performs the work has reviewed it and material cross-team handoffs have been checked with the relevant participant.

## Discovery sequence

Use the following order because it begins with the strongest candidate end-to-end workflows and lets cross-cutting practices emerge naturally.

1. Observe the Customer Data and Systems Support officer completing a duplicate-record work episode.
2. Observe the Customer Focus Information Support officer completing a Service Directory update.
3. Review the artefacts and system behaviours associated with those two episodes.
4. Run short follow-up conversations to capture exceptions, variations, controls and work not seen during observation.
5. Speak with one internal customer for each observed workflow to test the request, handoff and outcome from the other side.
6. Complete the cleansing and correction cluster and the urgent and hot-topic information cluster using recent examples.
7. Test the remaining monitoring, maintenance, tool and support candidates against evidence of actual frequency and distinct workflow boundaries.
8. Consolidate, reframe or remove candidates before marking any SOP `In review`.

## Observation capture prompts

Use these prompts while watching a real task:

1. What caused you to start this task?
2. Where did the request or work item appear?
3. What did you check before acting?
4. Which information or system did you trust, and why?
5. Where did you make a judgement rather than follow a documented rule?
6. What would make you stop or escalate?
7. What did you update, record or communicate?
8. How did you know the task was complete?
9. What was different about this example from the usual process?
10. What would another person need to know to complete the same task safely?

## Informal officer conversation prompts

Use real examples rather than asking the officer to design an ideal process.

- Show me the last time you completed this work.
- What are the common variations?
- Which cases take disproportionate effort?
- What do you know that is not written down?
- What do people often misunderstand about your role in this process?
- What are you expected to own but cannot control?
- What work arrives through relationships rather than a formal channel?
- What happens when you are unavailable?
- Which check would create the greatest risk if it were skipped?
- Which parts are stable enough to document, and which are still inconsistent?

## Internal-customer conversation prompts

- What do you ask this function to help with?
- How do you know where or to whom to send the request?
- What information do you provide?
- What response or outcome do you expect?
- Where do requests slow down, bounce or require clarification?
- What depends on the officer's personal knowledge or relationships?
- How do you know that the issue or update is complete?
- What happens when the function is unavailable?
- What would make the service more reliable without adding unnecessary process?
