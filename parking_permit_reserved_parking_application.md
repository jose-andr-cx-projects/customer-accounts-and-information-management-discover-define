## Permit type decision

### Use a Skip Bin Permit when

A **Skip Bin Permit** is required when the item is **not a vehicle**, including when:

- wheels do not touch the road;
- a container or bin is left directly on the street;
- a crane or scissor lift occupies the road or footpath; or
- the item is otherwise not a registered vehicle.

The company providing the bin or container should apply for the relevant permit.

### Use a Reserved Parking Permit when

A **Reserved Parking Permit** is required when:

- the item is a registered vehicle;
- a storage container is mounted on a registered trailer;
- the trailer's wheels are touching the road; or
- parking bays need to be reserved or kept vacant.

For a resident using a trailer-based storage solution, the resident should apply for the permit.

## Important conditions

- A City of Melbourne Services Account is **not required** for this permit type.
- This permit is issued by the **Permits Team**, not the Town Hall Service Desk.
- There are **no interim permits** for this permit type.
- There is **no renewal** process. A new application is required.

## Fees

### Standard reserved parking fees

- **$206 per day** for the first parking bay.
- **$103 per day** for each additional bay.

A day is measured from **00:01 to 23:59**.

### Processing notice and priority fees

The normal processing requirement is **five clear business days' notice**.

Applications submitted with four clear business days or less before the required start date may be accepted at the Permits Team's discretion.

Priority processing fees:

| Notice provided | Priority processing fee |

| Five business days | $206 |
| Four business days | $306 |
| Three business days | $412 |

## Resident discount

A 50 per cent discount applies to residents who require reserved parking outside their home for:

- building maintenance;
- renovations; or
- a removalist.

Discounted fees:

- **$103 per day** for the first parking bay.
- **$51.50 per day** for each additional bay.

To qualify, the resident must provide one of the following:

- a current utility bill showing their name and supply address;
- a welcome letter from a utility provider showing their name and supply address; or
- a tenancy letter.

## Service description

The reserved parking scheme allows residents and businesses to book parking bays within the City of Melbourne municipality.

It may also apply to storage containers or bins mounted on trailers or wheels.

If the item is not on wheels, the customer must apply for a Skip Bin Permit instead.

Reserved parking may be useful for:

- special events;
- filming, with a mud map supplied;
- construction or maintenance;
- weddings, including access for the bride or guests with limited mobility;
- loading or unloading equipment;
- removalists;
- school and community groups using buses; and
- funeral services.

### Funeral services

For funeral services:

- the Permits Team does not require the usual five-day application notice;
- late lodgement fees are not applied; and
- the customer should apply online or use the application form and note the actual date the permit is required.

## Booking hours

Reserved parking permits can be booked from **5:00 am onward** and may run until **11:59 pm**.

## Managing reserved parking permits

### 1. Signs unchanged - self-managed locations

Where signs cannot be changed:

1. locate a permissive, green-signed parking bay;
2. park in the bay;
3. display the permit clearly on the dashboard; and
4. ensure all permit details remain visible and legible.

Permit holders may use bollards or witches hats to reserve the approved bay or bays in accordance with the permit.

### 2. Temporary sign changes

Where signs can be changed, temporary signs may state:

> No Stopping - Reserved Parking Permit Holders Excepted

When arriving:

1. locate the signed bay or bays;
2. park within the designated area; and
3. display the permit clearly on the dashboard with all inscriptions legible.

## Vehicles, trailers, containers and skip bins

Reserved parking cannot be used for non-vehicles such as:

- skip bins;
- unmounted containers;
- cranes; or
- scissor lifts.

The City of Melbourne issues separate permits for occupation of road or footpath space for these activities.

### Registered trailer rule

A storage container mounted on a registered trailer and parked on the road is treated as a registered vehicle.

A Reserved Parking Permit should be used when parking spaces need to be reserved.

Once the trailer is removed and the container remains on the street, the item is no longer considered a vehicle and a Skip Bin Permit is required.

### TaxiBox example

A TaxiBox is a container rather than a registered motor vehicle.

A Reserved Parking Permit does not apply unless the container is mounted on a registered trailer that remains with it.

### Vehicle definition recorded in the article

The article states that a vehicle includes:

- a motor vehicle, trailer and tram;
- a bicycle; and
- an animal-drawn vehicle, or an animal being ridden or drawing a vehicle.

### Example outcomes

- **Example 1:** container mounted on a registered trailer — Reserved Parking Permit.
- **Example 2:** trailer removed and container left on the road — Skip Bin Permit.

## Contractor signage

### New reserved parking signs for contractor works

Temporary signs are used in residential areas where parking needs to be reserved for Citywide or City of Melbourne contractor works.

The sign wording is:

> No Stopping - Authorised Council Contractors Excepted

The signs were introduced to prevent residential parking permit holders from assuming that a residential permit allowed them to park in contractor work areas.

The article states that a trial of this wording was successful and that the signs were rolled out for:

- Citywide;
- Peritus; and
- Duncan Solutions.

Contractors still apply for and display Reserved Parking Permits, but the permit authorises them to park or work within areas signed:

> No Stopping - Authorised Council Contractors Excepted

## Work area and application follow-up

Applications are processed according to the **date required**, not the date received.

Staff should refer to the Permits Team's daily statistics email to confirm the date currently being processed.

For application enquiries, ask for:

- the date required;
- the customer's name; and
- the Service Request number, where the booking was submitted online.
